FROM THE STUDIO

BUILT BY DESIGN,
NOT BY BUDGET

Why one person uses AI to make games, and why that should not be controversial.

A note from the creator of Brok3n by Design

The quiet gate

Here is something the industry does not like to say out loud: for most of its history, making a content-rich game has been a question of money.

Think about what a finished game actually holds. Thousands of voice lines. Hundreds of unique images. Systems, menus, music, and the small details that make a world feel alive instead of empty. Producing that much has always taken capital. A publisher. A funded team. Or the ability to work for years without a real paycheck.

Talent mattered. Vision mattered. They were rarely enough on their own. The real filter was who could afford to keep going.

That filter decides which games get finished. Nobody designed it, but it works like a gate. Plenty of good ideas never reach players because the person behind them ran out of money before they ran out of ideas.

What changed

I'm one person. I design, direct, and build every Brok3n by Design project myself, between a full-time tech job and being a dad. There is no art department down the hall. There is no voice booth, no QA team, no publisher writing checks.

A few years ago that would have meant one of two things: make something small, or make something that never gets done.

AI-assisted tools changed that math. Voice generation, image tools, and code assistance let me produce and iterate on the volume of content a real game needs, without a six-figure budget or a decade of nights and weekends. The gap between a clear idea and a finished, content-dense game is narrower than it has ever been.

That is not hype. It is the only reason some of these projects exist at all.

What did not change

The tools do not have taste. They do not know what belongs in a world and what does not. They will happily generate a thousand things, and nine hundred of them will be wrong.

So the job is the same job it always was. I decide what each game is, how it plays, and what belongs in it.

WRITING & VOICE

I write the lines

Every line is mine. AI performs it. I direct the read and throw out the takes that miss.

ART

I set the look

Every image starts from my concept and direction. I redraw, edit, or cut whatever does not fit the world.

CODE

I design the systems

AI helps me write and debug faster. It does not decide what the game is.

It is the same loop I call Flow Programming on the About page. I just get through it more times.

Design. Build. Play. Refine. Same loop. More passes.

Why I'm saying this

People argue about AI in games, and some of the criticism is fair. Low-effort work exists. It always has, with every new tool.

But a lot of the argument quietly assumes the old way was fine. It was not. "Only people with deep pockets can make big games" was never a healthy rule, and I am not going to defend it out of nostalgia.

The means of making things should not be reserved for people who already have money. A better creative landscape is one where more people with skill and drive actually get to finish what they started. These tools make that possible for me. I am not embarrassed by that. I am proud of what one person can build now.

One creative vision, carried through every iteration. The tools changed. The vision did not.
